From mood board to firmware

One design system, eight devices.

A product is designed before it is programmed. This page follows one family of devices from its first mood boards to screens that run on a microcontroller, with lvgl26 reading the design system's tokens at compile time.

The path

  1. Research. What each device demands: a thermostat read from across a room, a watch read in a second, a machine panel where colour is kept for alarms, a charger that must show its price, a medical screen whose alarms have priorities.
  2. Mood boards. Three directions for the whole family. One was chosen: Signal.
  3. Design system. Signal's tokens and rules, below. Done.
  4. Screens. The key screens of all eight devices, drawn with Signal. In progress.
  5. Firmware. The same tokens, read by lvgl26 at compile time; the screens as compile-time trees; sizes measured on a Cortex-M4. Planned.

The devices: smart thermostat, smartwatch, kitchen appliance, e-bike display, machine panel, EV charger, medical device, vehicle instrument cluster.

Signal

Black ground, tall condensed numbers, square corners, and one loud colour that marks what the person chose. A value in its normal range has no colour at all, so that an alarm is seen.

Rules made for a microcontroller

  • Fill, do not blend. Square blocks are the fastest thing to draw. There are no shadows, gradients or blur.
  • Large numbers get a font of their own that holds only the digits and a few signs.
  • Redraw the number, not the screen. A reading sits in its own block, so only that block changes.
  • Touch targets are tokens: 48 px for a bare finger, 80 px where gloves are worn.

The files

The design system is in the repository, under the MIT licence like everything else:

  • design/signal/README.md: the rules, written so that a person or an AI agent can design a new screen with them.
  • design/signal/tokens.design.json: the tokens as the design tool keeps them. lvgl26 reads design tokens in the W3C format; the converter between the two is the next step.
